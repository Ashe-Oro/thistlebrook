# OPUS-TESLA decisions log (append-only)

Spec: docs/specs/OPUS-TESLA-LINEUP.md. Branch village/opus-tesla.

## 2026-11-17

1. **Baseline.** Full suite, `test:checks`, `audit:roads` (0 open) and `sim:collisions` (all zero) pass before any change. Lineup cost at the arena camera: 120 meshes / 120 draw calls (`shots/opus-tesla/metrics-before.json`), frame 597 calls.
2. **Reference photos are local only.** 17 Wikimedia Commons photos (CC BY / BY-SA / CC0) are in `docs/specs/tesla-refs/`, but the JPGs are gitignored because main is published to GitHub and the spec says never to ship photos. `SOURCES.md` (URL, author, licence) is committed, so the set can be fetched again.
3. **The Roadster is untouched.** It keeps its original factory path in `models.js`. Only the five lineup kinds (`sedan`=Model 3, `crossover`=Model Y, `cybercab`, `cybertruck`, `semi`) move to the new `lineup-models.js`. The same factory still feeds the town traffic adapter and preview fleet, so traffic gets the new cars automatically.
4. **One loft builder, vertex colours.** Each body is a loft of mirrored cross-sections, with each face painted by zone (hood, windshield, roof, rear glass, deck…) and face type (lower, side, glass, roof). One toon mesh holds the body and details, one indexed smooth-normal ink hull holds the outline, front and tail lamps are one emissive mesh each, plus one mesh per wheel. That is 8 draw calls per car and 10 for the Semi, against 20–27 before.
5. **`semi.js` deleted.** The Semi is now a design in the same builder: no trailer, because none is in the lineup.
6. **Rest stop.** Ashe's screenshot comes from two separate generators: `stop-roadster`, the `district-stop-roadster` scene (bench, crate, barrel, sand pad and its three catalog footpaths), and the arena's own "campus walk" connector box in `arena.js`, which lay across the east world-link road's crosswalk. I removed the scene entry plus its recipe and group, and the connector box. The other 8 district stops are unchanged; the registry test now expects 8 and asserts that the Roadster district has none.
7. **Blind critic round 1** (shuffled letters, refs labelled, renders unlabelled): 5/5 correct, but Model 3 and Model Y were 40% and Cybercab 60%, because the three shared one side silhouette. Fixes taken: Model Y is taller with a higher ride, a roof peak over the rear seats, a near-vertical liftgate and dark cladding; Model 3 is lower, a fastback to a short trunk lip with long swept headlamps; Cybercab is one continuous fastback with the windshield further forward and bigger thin-tyre discs; Semi has a rounder protruding nose, a more raked wraparound glass, corner headlamps instead of a bar, and bigger wheels; Cybertruck gets a steel band above the side glass, a thinner black rocker and chunkier tyres.
8. **Blind critic round 2** (new shuffle, new critic): 5/5 correct. Cybertruck 97%, Semi 98%, Model Y 75%, Model 3 60%, Cybercab 60%. Real glitches it found: the Cybertruck bed-edge trim floated above the tailgate; the Model 3 lip block and rotated tail lamps stuck out; the Semi had a pale paint triangle above its side glass; and fan-triangulated front caps painted a huge black chin. All fixed: caps are paint, bumpers are explicit strips, the Semi glass wraps fully, and the trim and lip were removed. The Cybercab tail was too high (it read as a hatchback), so it is now one long fastback falling to a low tail.
9. **Ring grew to 10 points.** A window-top point splits the greenhouse into glass and an upper band (black rail on Model 3/Y, steel band on Cybertruck), which the critic asked for.
10. **Blind critic round 3**: 5/5 correct. Cybertruck 97%, Semi 98%, Cybercab 85%, Model Y 80%, Model 3 78%. The "dark blocks on the Cybercab hood" and the "floating Model Y mirror" were wheel-arch plates rising above the hood line, so arches are now clipped below the local belt line. Also taken: Model 3 cowl moved back with a shorter windshield; Model Y gets a thin rocker instead of heavy cladding and its tail bar moves to the beltline; Cybercab sits lower with a longer low hood and slightly smaller discs; Cybertruck side glass ends sooner under a deeper steel band and the chin tab is gone; Semi windshield is taller; tyres have 20 segments.
11. **Capture under load.** The shared machine sat at load average 70–140 during this work, so the capture tool now waits up to 600 s for `__ready` and 300 s per screenshot, and takes `--kinds` to re-shoot single cars.
12. **Blind critic round 4**: 5/5 correct. Semi 98%, Cybertruck 98%, Cybercab 85%, Model Y 75% (round 3: 80%), Model 3 70% (round 3: 78%). Following "revert anything worse", I reverted the round-4 Model 3 cowl move and restored the round-3 Model Y lower cladding (the replacement thin stripe read as a mid-body band). I kept the glitch fixes the critic confirmed: smaller door mirrors (the 0.1 m cubes read as floating blocks), the Model Y tail bar moved down onto the liftgate (it read as roof-corner lamps), no wheel-arch plates on the Cybercab (clipped arches left dark slivers on the hood; the real car has body-colour arches), a thin full-length Cybercab sill instead of a short block, a rounder Cybercab roof (wider top and one more station), and 10-sided Cybertruck aero covers instead of hexagons.
13. **Round 5 regression caught.** The full-length Cybercab sill crossed the wheel discs, so it now runs only between the wheels.
14. **Blind critic round 5** (shuffle 41): 5/5 correct. Semi 98%, Cybertruck 97%, Cybercab 80%, Model 3 65%, Model Y 65%. Across five independent critics and five shuffles, every vehicle was named correctly every time (25/25). Model 3/Y confidence moves between 60% and 80% from critic to critic: they share Tesla's sedan/crossover family and differ mainly in height, roof length and light signature, which matches the real cars. Last glitch fixes, both removals: the door mirrors (every critic read them as floating cubes) and the Model Y liftgate spoiler block (read as red blocks sticking up). I stopped iterating here: the identification target holds and the remaining notes are taste (rounder roofs) that the low-poly loft can't take without blowing the triangle budget or the shared look.
15. **Final results.** Lineup draw calls at the arena camera fall from 120 to 42 (−78; the budget allowed up to +25), and the whole arena frame falls from 597 to 508 calls. Triangles per vehicle (body + hull + lamps + wheels): Cybercab 1,548, Cybertruck 1,248, Semi 2,054, Model Y 1,800, Model 3 1,804, all within 3,000. The Roadster still clears every lineup car by more than 6 m (`tests/astra-tesla.test.mjs`, unchanged jump). `audit:roads`: 0 open. `sim:collisions`: all zero. Full suite: 171/171. `test:checks`: passes. The Roadster district has no catalog scene (`metrics-after.json` `reststop: []`). Evidence is in `shots/opus-tesla/`. `metrics-before.json` lineup fields were restored from the first baseline run's printed output, because a later cars-only re-capture overwrote the file.
